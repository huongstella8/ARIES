The key tense of this module: present perfect passive

RuleWhen you write about changes between a time in the past and NOW, the main tense is the present perfect passive:
A new tram line has been built… · …a bike-rental scheme has been introduced…
⚠ But these can NEVER be passive: take place, become, change, make way for.
A number of important changes have taken place in the last decade. · The city has become much more modern. · The cinema was knocked down to make way for a new shopping centre.

Coherence vs Cohesion — the two C's of your mark

CoherenceCohesion
Is it easy to understand? Are ideas organised in a logical way (intro → what changed → what stayed the same → summary)?Are your ideas connected well — linking devices, pronouns, articles, synonyms — so the text flows without too many short sentences?

Grammar point 2 · Present perfect passive (past → now)

Formhas/have + been + past participle — A new tram line has been built.
⚠ No-passive verbs: take place · become · change · make way for → use active present perfect: changes have taken place, the city has become more modern.

Task Achievement · Select and report the main features

The question itself tells you what to do: "Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant." For every feature decide: report individually (a major change), group together (similar or unchanged features share one sentence), or leave out (irrelevant detail).

🎯 Examiner insight: unchanged features deserve one combined sentence, not four separate ones: "The church, pond, windmill and bridge, however, all remain unchanged."
